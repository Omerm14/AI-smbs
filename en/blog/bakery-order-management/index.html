How to manage bakery orders without losing your morning to WhatsApp

By Omer Moran, Founder & Principal, AAA · Published July 16, 2026

If your bakery's orders arrive by phone, WhatsApp, and a shared spreadsheet, the fix isn't a fancier spreadsheet — it's getting every order onto one production list automatically, the moment it's placed.

Why this breaks down specifically in a bakery

A bakery's order flow is unusually fragmented compared to most retail businesses. A regular client texts an order on WhatsApp at 6am. A new client calls it in. A wholesale account submits a recurring weekly order by email. Someone on staff then has to notice each one, decide what it means for tomorrow's production, and copy it into a list — by hand, under time pressure, before the ovens start. Every manual copy step is a place a quantity gets mistyped, an order gets missed, or the list is already out of date by the time production actually starts.

What actually needs to happen, step by step

  1. Orders land in one place automatically — whether the client used a portal or just messaged on WhatsApp, the order shouldn't require someone to transcribe it.
  2. The production list builds itself — quantities and items aggregate directly from the orders received, not from someone's memory of what came in.
  3. Packing and delivery status update in the same system — so "is this order ready?" has one answer, not three people's guesses.
  4. Everything becomes searchable history — so next week's "what did they order last time?" is a lookup, not a memory test.

Generic order software vs. an AI-native fit for how you already work

Most order-management platforms solve this by asking the bakery's clients to adopt a new ordering system — a portal, an app, a specific workflow. That's a real cost: clients who are used to just texting the order now have to change their own habit, and adoption is never 100%. The alternative is meeting clients exactly where they already are:

  • Generic order software — requires the client to use the platform's interface; strong reporting, but adoption depends on changing the client's behavior.
  • An AI-native system built around WhatsApp — reads the order wherever the client actually sent it, with no new habit required on their end, and still produces the same structured production list on your side.
