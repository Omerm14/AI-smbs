Studios & creative businesses

Your studio's knowledge is buried in old files. A voice AI can answer instead.

By Omer Moran, Founder & Principal, AAA · Published July 16, 2026

Years of quotes, briefs, and specs shouldn't require someone remembering exactly which folder they're in. A voice AI that sits on top of a studio's own history can answer a spoken question directly — and cite the actual document behind the answer.

Why this is worse in a studio specifically

A retail or food business's data is fairly structured — orders, invoices, inventory. A studio or creative business's knowledge is the opposite: years of quotes, creative briefs, technical specs, and client history, none of it filed the same way twice, scattered across email threads, shared drives, and whoever happened to be on the project at the time. "What was the spec for that installation two years ago?" or "How did we price something similar to this before?" become questions that depend entirely on someone remembering, or being willing to dig.

What actually needs to happen

  1. The existing knowledge gets indexed — quotes, briefs, specs, and past creative work, as they already exist, not re-entered into a new system.
  2. A spoken question gets a spoken answer — asked out loud, in conversation, the way someone would ask a colleague.
  3. Every answer cites its source — so the answer is checkable against the actual document, not a guess.
  4. Patterns surface on their own — places to streamline or improve become visible because the knowledge is finally searchable as a whole, not spread across years of folders.

File search vs. an agent that understands the question

  • Keyword file search — finds documents containing the words you typed, but leaves you to read through results and piece together the answer yourself.
  • A voice AI agent — understands what's actually being asked, pulls from multiple sources if needed, and gives you the answer directly, with the source cited so you can verify it.
